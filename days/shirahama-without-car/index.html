Two days in Shirahama without a car

A slower itinerary for white sand, historic hot springs and dramatic Pacific coastline.

By Shirahama Days editorial team · Sources checked 19 November 2026 · Desk-researched guide

Quick answer: Base yourself near Shirarahama Beach or Yuzaki, use local buses for longer coastal journeys and leave time between connections. This itinerary is a suggested plan, not a fixed timetable.

Getting to Shirahama

Arrive by JR train from Osaka or the Kumano region, or fly into Nanki-Shirahama Airport. The station and airport are separate from the central beach area. Check the local bus route or ask your accommodation about a shuttle. If arriving from the pilgrimage trail, read our Kumano Kodo to Shirahama guide.

Day one: beach and hot springs

Morning or early afternoon — Shirarahama Beach

Make the white-sand beach your first stop. Even outside the swimming season, a stroll allows you to enjoy the coastal scenery. During the supervised swimming season, follow local signs, flags and lifeguard instructions. The beach is public; current rental and facility details should be checked locally.

Afternoon — Yuzaki and the hot springs

Head towards the historic Yuzaki hot spring area. Saki-no-Yu is known for its ocean-facing bathing setting. Check the latest opening hours, bathing rules and weather-related closures before visiting. Private bathing arrangements may suit travellers who prefer more privacy.

Evening — sunset and dinner

Leave the evening free for a walk, a meal featuring local seafood or an evening at your accommodation. Check restaurant opening days and reserve independently if you have dietary requirements or a specific restaurant in mind.

Day two: coastal landscapes

Morning — Senjojiki and Sandanbeki

Use the local bus to explore the rocky coastal scenery. Senjojiki has broad layered rock formations, while Sandanbeki is known for its cliffs. Stay within permitted viewing areas and keep away from exposed rocks during rough weather.

Afternoon — Engetsu Island viewpoint

If transport and weather permit, head towards a viewing point for Engetsu Island before returning to the beach or your accommodation. The island's natural arch is a distinctive feature of Shirahama's coastline. Avoid rushing for sunset if the return bus connection is uncertain.

If it rains

Slow down with a longer hot spring visit, an indoor meal or a suitable indoor attraction. Check whether a facility is open before setting off. Avoid the cliff and exposed coastal rock areas during storms. If you have just finished a long hike, a flexible day at your accommodation may be the best option.

Where to base yourself

Near Shirarahama Beach is convenient for a beach-focused stay; Yuzaki is a good alternative for historic bathing and coastal access. Choose accommodation near the railway station if an early departure matters most, but remember that you will have to travel to the beach area. Book directly with the accommodation or through an authorised booking platform.

Frequently asked questions

Is Shirahama worth visiting outside summer?

Yes, if you are interested in hot springs, coastal scenery and a quieter pace. Swimming services and some seasonal activities may be unavailable.

Can I do this as a day trip?

A shorter day trip can work, but an overnight stay gives you more flexibility for buses, meals and bathing. Two days allow you to avoid rushing.

Do hot springs accept tattoos?

Policies vary by facility and can change. Ask the individual bath or accommodation in advance; do not assume one location's policy applies to another.
