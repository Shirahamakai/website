Shirahama in winter: a coastal hot spring escape

When the beach season ends, discover the warmth of one of Japan's historic hot spring towns.

By Shirahama Days editorial team · Sources checked 19 November 2026 · Desk-researched guide

Why visit in winter?

Shirahama is known for its white-sand beach, but its hot spring culture predates modern seaside tourism. Visit Wakayama identifies Shirahama Onsen alongside Dogo and Arima as one of Japan's three oldest hot spring areas. Cooler weather makes a warm bath and a leisurely meal especially appealing. Take a coastal walk when conditions are calm, then return to the warmth of your accommodation.

Seaside hot springs to explore

Saki-no-Yu

An open-air bath facing the Pacific Ocean, where the waves form part of the setting. Check opening hours, closure days and bathing conditions directly before visiting.

Official Saki-no-Yu information

Shirasuna

The beachside facility offers a foot bath in winter according to the prefectural tourism guide. Check seasonal arrangements before setting out.

Official Shirasuna information

Stay at a ryokan or resort hotel

Consider accommodation with an on-site hot spring, particularly if you want to bathe on a flexible schedule or spend the evening enjoying a traditional meal. Private and public bath arrangements vary by property. Check privacy, tattoo and dietary policies before booking.

Regional accommodation directory

A relaxed two-day winter itinerary

Day one

Arrive by train or bus, check into your accommodation, explore the Yuzaki hot spring area, and enjoy a local seafood dinner. Leave time for a soak before bed.

Day two

Visit Saki-no-Yu if it is open, take a coastal stroll and explore Toretore Market for regional food. On windy or rainy days, adjust the itinerary in favour of indoor attractions and bathing. Check local transport before setting off.

Combine winter onsen with Kumano

Shirahama can serve as a coastal break before or after exploring the Kii Peninsula. Mountain conditions in winter can vary; consult official trail guidance and weather forecasts before choosing a walk.
