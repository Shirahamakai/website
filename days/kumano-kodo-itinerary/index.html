KUMANO KODO · PLANNING

Kumano Kodo itinerary: choose between 3, 4 and 6 days

How long should you spend on the Kumano Kodo? Start by deciding whether you want a continuous mountain walk or a selection of shorter pilgrimage sections. The Nakahechi is a network of connected trails, not a single itinerary that everyone has to complete.

By Shirahama Days editorial team · Sources checked 22 November 2026 · Desk-researched guide

Quick answer: Choose your route and confirmed beds first; keep onward travel and an optional coastal rest separate from walking days.

Compare the time you need

These options are adapted from the tourism bureau’s official models. Total days include arrival and onward travel, not only walking.

Three days: selected walks

Travel to the Hongu area on the first day. On the second day, walk from Hosshinmon-oji towards Kumano Hongu Taisha, then travel to Katsuura. On day three, visit the Nachisan area and walk the historic Daimon-zaka approach if conditions and transport allow.

This option suits travellers who want to experience the pilgrimage landscape without committing to consecutive long mountain days.

Four days: Takijiri to Hongu

One official model follows successive walking stages through Takahara and the Chikatsuyu/Nonaka area, arriving at Hongu on the third walking day. Day four is for onward travel. Secure accommodation at suitable stages before committing to the route.

Six days: continue towards Nachi

The longer official model adds the Kogumotori-goe and Ogumotori-goe mountain sections beyond Hongu. Allow time for the additional terrain, weather and limited rural accommodation. It is a different undertaking from the shorter highlights trip.

Do not compress the stages simply because accommodation is unavailable. Check the official distance and elevation charts, your own fitness, trail conditions and lodging before deciding.

Leave a day for the transition

If you have time after the walk, choose between another night near the shrines, the harbour at Katsuura or a coastal stay in Shirahama. Shirahama works particularly well as a separate rest day if you are heading towards the western side of the peninsula; it may be an unnecessary detour if your plans take you east.
