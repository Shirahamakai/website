From the Kumano Kodo to Shirahama

How to get to the coast after your pilgrimage, and make the most of a well-earned break.

By Shirahama Days editorial team · Sources checked 23 November 2026 · Desk-researched guide

Quick answer: If your walk ends near Kumano Hongu Taisha, check the bus to Shirahama. If you finish near Nachi, travel via Kii-Katsuura and the JR Kisei Line. From Kii-Tanabe, Shirahama is a short coastal journey. Confirm current schedules before travelling.

Where should you unwind after the Kumano Kodo?

Leave room to enjoy finishing your walk before the next long journey. Choose a place that suits your actual endpoint and onward route.

Shirahama is an optional extension. If you finish near Nachi and continue east, Katsuura may avoid a detour.

Five checks before adding a coastal night

Use your own itinerary to decide whether another stop is worthwhile. Complete these checks before reserving a room.

  1. Finish point: Hongu, Nachi or Kii-Katsuura? Start with the route that matches your actual last stop.
  2. Next destination: compare staying near your finish point with travelling to Shirahama. Do not add a coastal transfer simply to follow a suggested itinerary.
  3. Available nights: choose one night for a pause, or two if you want a full day without moving accommodation.
  4. Arrival and dinner: check the last usable connection and the property’s check-in and meal deadlines together.
  5. Luggage: confirm where your bags will be, when they arrive and whether the property can receive them.

Check the official bus information for your travel date, then compare Shirahama accommodation areas if the journey fits. This checklist is a planning suggestion; transport, meals and luggage services are not booked or guaranteed by us.

Start with the Kumano Tourism Bureau's bus timetables and Meiko Bus suggested routes. For rail journeys, use the JR West website. Services and timetables may change by season; do not rely on old screenshots.

What about your luggage?

Arrange luggage delivery before starting the trail. The Kumano Tourism Bureau's daily shuttle has specific booking conditions, including accommodation booked through its reservation platform. A different provider or advance delivery may be needed for a coastal hotel. Confirm your final bag collection point and delivery date before travelling.

One night or two?

One night: an easy reset

Travel to Shirahama after your walk, check in, enjoy a short seaside stroll and use your accommodation's bathing facilities if available. The following morning, visit Shirarahama Beach or a public hot spring before your onward train or flight.

Two nights: a slower coastal escape

Use the first afternoon for recovery. On your full day, explore the beach and coastal viewpoints, leave time for a hot spring and enjoy a relaxed dinner. Avoid packing too many stops into the day if you are tired from hiking.

Next step: Compare accommodation areas, then check official bus timetables and your chosen property's own booking conditions. For a quieter cultural afternoon, see our Minakata Kumagusu Museum guide.

Where to stay

Compare accommodation near Shirarahama Beach for convenient seaside access, Yuzaki for the historic hot spring area, or near Shirahama Station if an early train is your priority. Check the hotel's own website for meals, baggage policies and bathing arrangements before booking independently.

Frequently asked questions

Can I go directly from Hongu to Shirahama?

Bus services link the Hongu area with Shirahama and Tanabe. Check the timetable for your exact date and whether a transfer is necessary.

Do I need a car?

No car is necessary for many travellers, provided you plan bus and railway connections carefully. Our next guide covers an itinerary based on public transport.

Can I swim year-round?

Do not assume that swimming is supervised year-round. Check the current beach opening period, weather and local safety instructions before entering the sea.
